1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer: Simple interest for late payment at a rate equal to the reference
   rate plus at least eight percentage points.

   Source: Source 1, Article 2(6).

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer: No. The supplied directive does not establish a EUR 25 maximum
   late fee. Where late-payment interest becomes payable under Article 3
   or 4, it provides for a minimum fixed sum of EUR 40 as compensation for
   recovery costs. It also provides for reasonable compensation for
   recovery costs exceeding that sum and incurred due to late payment.
   This is recovery-cost compensation, not an arbitrary late fee.

   Source: Source 1, Article 6(1)–(3).

3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to
   interest and to the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer: No. In transactions between businesses, entitlement to
   late-payment interest does not require a reminder, provided the creditor
   has fulfilled its contractual and legal obligations and has not received
   payment on time, unless the debtor is not responsible for the delay.
   The fixed compensation sum is also payable without a reminder where
   interest becomes payable under Article 3 or 4.

   Source: Source 1, Article 3(1)(a)–(b), and Article 6(1)–(2).

4. What is the current ECB reference rate?

   Answer: Not in the sources. The directive defines the reference rate
   but gives no current numerical ECB rate. A dated official ECB publication
   identifying the relevant main refinancing operations rate would be needed.

   For transactions between businesses, the directive uses the rate in
   force on 1 January for the first half of the year and on 1 July for the
   second half. The applicable reference rate for October therefore requires
   the relevant rate in force on 1 July, rather than assuming today's rate
   applies.

   Source: Source 1, Article 2(7)(a)(i)–(ii), and Article 3(2)(a)–(b).
